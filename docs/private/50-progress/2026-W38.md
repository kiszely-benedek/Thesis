**Findings**
- **`tracemalloc` inflates the stage timings up to 9× — a near-miss that would have corrupted the kill
  criterion check.** The tool's first version measured time and memory in one pass. Measured at
  `n_units=50, budget=4, seed=0`: build 4.3×, loader 4.6×, **conceptual 9.1×**, adapter 4.1×, split
  2.9×; at 1,000 units the traced run totalled 89.99 s against 15.21 s untraced (**5.9×**). Since the
  criterion is a wall-clock threshold, filing the traced numbers would have made the pipeline look ~6×
  closer to failing than it is. **Caught by cross-checking a fresh number against the 2026-09-13
  prototype table** (untraced conceptual 0.378 s vs prototype 0.349 s; traced 3.424 s agrees with
  neither), not by reading the code. Fixed with `--no-memory` plus a `memory_traced` flag in the output;
  the inflation table now lives in the module docstring.
- **Step 6 was already complete** and nobody had noticed: `tests/test_generator_integration.py` covers
  invariant 7 (line 30), sheet-schema validity (line 45) and invariant 8 (line 57). Its only missing
  assertion was the §4.8 one, which fell with the datasheet decision. No work was needed.
- **The ontology question needed no position change.** The professor's "maybe overkill to use ontology
  for connecting sheets or putting them into hierarchy" is already satisfied by the code: the hierarchy
  and cross-sheet links are six plain terms in `graph/schema.py` (`DRAWING_SET`, `SHEET`, `HAS_SHEET`,
  `IS_DRAWN_ON`, `CONTINUES_AS`, `SAME_TAGGED_ITEM_AS`) plus `ConnectorPair` / `IdentityGroup`. We do not
  use an ontology to connect sheets and never did; the ontology strand is only ADR-0002's RDF arm.
- **§7's estimates held up** where they could be checked: ~1,370 sheets estimated vs **1,378** measured;
  15.114 s prototype vs **14.07 s**. Peak memory, previously "not measured", is now 278.1 MiB.
