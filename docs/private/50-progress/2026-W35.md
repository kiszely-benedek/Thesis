**Findings**

> ⚠ **Correction.** Earlier today this log recorded "OPEN100's 12 sheets are unrelated drawings." That came from a verbal report that had not actually been checked, and I wrote it into four files as verified fact. It was wrong. Corrected below after inspecting the files directly. Lesson: do not record a finding as verified unless it was verified — ask, or check it myself.

- **OPEN100's 12 sheets ARE one plant.** All are `PROJECT: OPEN 100` (Energy Impact Center nuclear design), same author "MAJ", same date 09/01/2020, revision A. Four systems span two sheets each: Reactor Coolant (PID 100), Residual Heat Removal (120), Feedwater (170), Aux Cooling Water (210).
- **They carry real, machine-resolvable off-page connectors.** Incoming on the left edge, outgoing on the right, each naming target sheet *and grid cell* — `PID 120-1 (D-1) RHRS` — plus a line number `SIZE-RCS-100007-SPEC-HC-X`. The sheets have A–H / 1–12 grid borders, so the coordinate resolves. Sheet 5 → `RCS-PID-100-2` is sheet 6.
- References resolving inside the set: PID 100-2, 110-1, 120-1, 150-1, 170-1, 180-1. Dangling outside it: PID **190**, **240**, **290**, plus PID 170 sheet 2 of 2 is absent.
- **The GraphML captures none of it.** Node attrs are only `label` + bbox; edge attr only `edge_label`, always `solid`. Vocabulary: connector 2408, crossing 734, arrow 451, instrumentation 350, valve 257, general 234, **inlet/outlet 96**, background 48, tank 35, pump 16. **No text, no tags, no equipment IDs anywhere.** Per-sheet nodes 181–675, mean 386 (paper says 382±125 — confirms it is the right data).
- **Therefore:** cross-sheet truth exists in the *images* and is missing from the *annotations*. Recovering it means annotating ~96 connector labels — days, not months. That would produce a real multi-sheet P&ID benchmark, which does not exist publicly today. The dangling PID 190/240/290 references are free, naturally occurring abstention cases.
- This also **calibrates the splitter**: real cut density, connectors per sheet (2–12), sheet sizes (181–675 nodes), and partition-by-system convention are now measurable rather than assumed.

**Spike result (this is what made ADR-0005 cheap)**
- Cropped the `inlet/outlet` bounding boxes on sheet 5. **The label text sits inside the box and is fully legible**; the line number sits just outside on the pipe and a ~3× width margin captures it. All 12 read correctly first try, with no OCR library and no training — a vision model reading montages is sufficient.
- Bounding boxes are in **image pixel space** (max = image dimensions), so no rescaling is needed.
- Two label grammars, and the asymmetry matters: **incoming** (left edge) is `PID <pid>-<sheet> (<grid>)` / `<SERVICE>` and carries the grid cell; **outgoing** (right edge) is `<SYSTEM NAME>` / `<SVC>-PID-<pid>-<sheet>` and does not. Pairing therefore keys on the **line number** (`SIZE-RHRS-121073-SPEC-HC-X`), confirmed by grid cell.
- Real formatting inconsistency observed (`PID-120-01` vs `PID 120-1`) — normalize, do not assume.

**Caught an invented claim — worth recording as a process note**
- A docstring in `models.py` asserted that a tag "can be written differently per sheet — `RC-P102A` and `RCS-PU-102A` are the same machine". The user challenged it: had I actually seen that case? **I had not.** I had conflated two real things into one unverified claim.
- Checked properly. What is true: `RCS-PU-102A` **does** appear on sheet 5 (process flow) *and* sheet 6 ("DETAIL A"), with `DET A SHT 2` callouts linking them — **real, verified cross-sheet tag identity.** Separately, sheet 5's symbol says `RCS-PU-102A` while its own equipment schedule says `RC-P102A` — **real spelling variation, but within one sheet.** Spelling varying *across* sheets was never observed.
- Docstring and design note corrected to say exactly this, with the unverified part marked as a hypothesis rather than a fact.
- **Consequence, and it is a good one: OPEN100 yields tag-identity ground truth too**, not just connector ground truth — at almost no extra cost, since the sheets are already open. Both go into the same manifest.
- Process lesson: CLAUDE.md's "never invent a number" applies to example data in docstrings just as much as to results tables. An illustrative example that was not observed is still a fabrication.
