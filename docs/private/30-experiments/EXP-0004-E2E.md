# P4: does a tag-variant resolver give the plant-scale accuracy back?

**Result (headline).** Tag variants with the exact resolver cost 12.5 points of accuracy (E1 PASS). The rule decider D2
wins 9.3 points back (E2 PASS) but stays 3.2 points under R5, so non-inferiority at the −0.03 margin fails narrowly
(E4 D2 FAIL, CI lower −0.055). The model decider D4 does not win a significant amount back (E3 FAIL) and stays 9.3
points under R5 (E4 D4 FAIL). D4 gains on the questions that cross a sheet through a variant tag, and loses on the
questions that do not: it merges items that are distinct.

Source: `data/runs/exp0004/e2e/report/report.md` and `report.json` (`python -m plantgraph.exp0004.e2e_report`).

## Setup

- System: V2T-A frozen (`cascade_v4_iso`, setting A, accuracy within 120 s), unchanged since R5.
- Corpus: `H1000g2V`, the sealed H1000g2 plant with tag variants on the items drawn on two sheets. The sealed
  H1000g2 question set rehomed onto it (proof file `e2e/<arm>/rehome_proof.json`).
- Arms: one load of `H1000g2V` per resolver link set, D0 (exact), D2 (rules, τ 0.857), D4 (GPT-6 Luna). Each arm ran
  both tiers (Cypher, agent) on all 560 questions, then a cache-only replay of each tier.
- Baseline: R5 `S7-{CYP,AG}-H1000g2` (EXP-0007-R5-sealed.md).
- Scope: R5's 440-question headline scope (dev-old + dev-new + held-out, without LOOP_ACTUATED_VALVE), paired by
  question id. Paired bootstrap and Wilson intervals from the EXP-0007 claims code, default seed and resamples.
- Order: free dry runs on a copy of the cache (2026-11-05), caps from their misses, then paid d0, d2, d4 one after
  the other on Neo4j database `e7tune` (2026-11-06 08:48 to 16:40). Scripts `run_e2e.sh`, `run_p4_rest*.sh`, log
  `e2e_paid.log`.

## Reading

- The identity problem is real at plant scale and sits where it should: on the 347 unexposed questions D0 equals R5
  (316 vs 318), on the 93 exposed questions it falls from 74 to 21.
- Rules (D2) are the best resolver end to end. They recover 37 of the 53 lost exposed answers and cost nothing on the
  unexposed ones. The missing 16 match P3: D2 resolves the format family fully and the naming family poorly (pair
  recall 0.223 on `H1000g2V`).
- The model decider (D4) recovers about as many exposed answers as D2 (55 vs 58), against its P3 pair recall of
  0.967. It loses 22 unexposed answers that R5 and D2 keep. The likely cause is false merges. D4's pair precision on
  `H1000g2V` was 0.828, D2's 0.993 (P3, `data/runs/exp0004/test/report.md`). A false merge joins two distinct items
  and creates a path that is not on the drawing: NO_PATH falls from 20 to 7, DOWNSTREAM_IN_UNIT from 16 to 6.
  This is read from the P3 precision and the family pattern. It is not checked question by question.
- So P3's ranking (D4 above D2 on F1, 0.892 vs 0.768 on `H1000g2V`) does not carry over to answers. Pair F1 weighs
  a false merge like a missed one, but a false merge does more harm downstream. For the thesis: an identity resolver
  for a question-answering graph must be judged on precision first, and the end-to-end measurement was needed to
  see it.
- D4 also costs more per answer (agent spend per missed question 0.00431 vs 0.00266 for D2). The merged items have
  more neighbours, so the agent's prompts grow. This too is an inference from the spend, not measured per prompt.

## Threats

- **Cypher-tier replays are not exact for d2 and d4.** The d0 replays and the d2 and d4 agent replays are identical.
  The d2 and d4 Cypher replays stop at a cache miss after 361 and 362 identical rows. Variable-length `line_to*1..`
  queries hit Neo4j's transaction timeout, so whether a query returns rows depends on timing, and a different
  result gives a different follow-up prompt. The scored answers are the first, paid run's.
- **Caps above the pre-registered rule.** The rule was misses × R5 cost per question × 1.5. The agent tier cost more
  than that (d2 1.42× R5's per-question figure, d4 more), so with the user's approval (2026-11-06) the caps were
  raised inside the $4.44 bound. This changes no answer, only how far a run could go before stopping. It is a
  deviation from PREREG section 7 and is reported as one.
- **Spend guard overcounts on a resume.** It adds the recorded cost of the rows on disk, cache hits included, so
  resumed tiers needed nominal caps far above their real spend (`run_p4_rest3.sh`, `run_p4_rest4.sh`). Real spend
  above is from `calls.jsonl`, calls not served from the cache.
- **One plant, one variant generator.** `H1000g2V` uses the generator's eight variant operators. A real plant's
  naming habits may favour rules or a model differently.
- **Resumes.** d2 and d4 each resumed twice after cap stops. Rows already on disk were kept, not re-asked.
- **E4's margin.** D2 misses the −0.03 margin by 0.025 on the CI lower bound. With 440 questions the interval is
  about ±0.023 wide. The point estimate −0.032 is itself just past the margin, so a larger set would not by itself
  turn this into a pass.
