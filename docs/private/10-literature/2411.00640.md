## Claim
"Fundamentally, evaluations are experiments" (Abstract). The paper gives analytic formulas for standard errors (naive and
clustered), paired model comparison, variance reduction and power. Its five recommendations are in §1.

## Method
- **§2:** questions "were drawn at random from a (hypothetical, infinite, unseen) super-population of questions".
  The score is s_i = x_i + ε_i (conditional mean plus zero-mean noise). Eq. 1 is the CLT standard error, Eq. 2 the
  Bernoulli one, Eq. 3 the 95% CI.
- **§2.1:** "we regard bootstrapping as unnecessary unless a complicated sampling scheme or estimator is being used."
- **§2.2:** for grouped questions (DROP, QuAC, RACE, SQuAD; MGSM translations) "a key assumption of the Central Limit
  Theorem (or a bootstrap) is violated". Eq. 4 is the clustered SE, and it assumes "that draws of clusters are
  independent". App. A derives it.
- **§3:** Var(μ̂) = (Var(x) + E[σ_i²])/n. Resampling K answers per question cuts only the second term (§3.1). Pooling
  all K·N answers into one SE "will be inconsistent". §3.3: "Don't touch the thermostat!"
- **§4:** unpaired SE_{A−B} = √(SE_A² + SE_B²) (§4.1). Paired differences (§4.2, Eq. 8 is the clustered version)
  "represent a “free” reduction in estimator variance".
- **§5:** Eq. 9 gives the sample size, Eq. 10 the minimum detectable effect (MDE). App. C gives cluster-adjusted
  versions and says to subsample whole clusters when estimating them.

## Evaluation
No new benchmark and no question set of its own.
- **Tables 1–3 and 5:** fictional models ("Galleon", "Dreadnought") with fictional numbers.
- **Table 4:** the only real data, "using Anthropic models (non-fictional numbers)". Model names, question counts and
  run counts are not given. The caption says "two popular evals", but the table has three rows.
  | Eval | SE clustered | SE CLT | Ratio |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | DROP | (1.34) | (0.44) | 3.05 |
  | RACE-H | (0.51%) | (0.46%) | 1.10 |
  | MGSM | (1.62%) | (0.86%) | 1.88 |
- **Power, worked example (§5):** with σ² = 0, ω² = 1/9, δ = 0.03, α = 0.05 and power 80%, n ≈ 969. Hence new evals
  "should contain at least 1,000 questions". With n = 198 and correlation 0.5, raising K from 1 to 10 lowers the MDE
  from 13.2% to 7.5%. The paper says itself that these parameters "are fictional".

**Claimed vs demonstrated.** The formulas are standard results (cited to the experiment-design literature). The only
empirical demonstration is Table 4, which has no question counts. The paper never discusses how many clusters
Eq. 4 needs. Its examples have many clusters (passages, languages).

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us a method and takes no territory.
- **Unit of analysis.** Our questions are a super-population sample (§2). The **plant** is the cluster, in the sense
  of §2.2. The family-within-plant draw is a second grouping that §2.2 also covers.
- **Why a bootstrap at all.** The two-stage cluster bootstrap over 5 plants per scale, with an equal-weight mean over
  plants, is the "complicated sampling scheme or estimator" case of §2.1. Cite that sentence as the reason we did not
  use Eq. 4.
- **Few clusters.** Eq. 4 needs independent clusters and, implicitly, many of them. At G = 5 it would be unreliable,
  and Miller says nothing about few clusters. That gap is filled by Cameron et al. (2008) and Saravanan et al.
  (`2007.07797.md`), not by this paper.
- **Paired arms** share one bootstrap draw. That is §4.2 / Eq. 8. Q4 (S1000 − S100) is unpaired, so §4.1 applies.
- **One run per question** is K = 1. §3.1 says this leaves E[σ_i²] in the variance. State it as a limitation.
- **Power.** Our 1,175 questions per scale (5 × 235, EXP-0009) exceed 969 only under independence. App. C's cluster
  terms would raise the requirement. Report the MDE per comparison (Eq. 10), not just the CIs.
- **Reporting.** Give the question count *and* the cluster count with every interval (Table 3 caption).
- It is not about the LLM-judge audit, the decoys or the gold.

## Limitations they admit
No limitations section. Caveats in the text: the parameters are "fictional, they are reasonable" (§5). App. C notes
that estimating the cluster terms needs prior data with K ≫ 1.

## Reusable
Eq. 4 (clustered SE, as a cross-check against the bootstrap), Eq. 8 (paired and clustered), Eqs. 9–10 (sample size,
MDE), and the reporting format of Tables 2, 3 and 5: n in each cell, SE in parentheses, pairwise Δ with its SE,
CI and correlation.
