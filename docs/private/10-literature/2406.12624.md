## Claim
In a "fairly sterile" reference-based setup, only the largest judges align well with humans, and they still differ by
"up to 5 points" from human scores. Percent agreement hides this, and Scott's π shows it. Judges show "a bias toward
leniency" (Abstract, §6).

## Method
- **Task:** TriviaQA (unfiltered validation set), judged against its short reference answers.
- **Exam-takers:** 9 models (Llama-2 7/13/70B base and chat, Mistral 7B base and chat, GPT-4 Turbo).
- **Judges:** 13 in total (§1). These are 11 LLMs (Llama-2/3/3.1, Gemma 2B, Mistral 7B, JudgeLM 7B, GPT-4 Turbo;
  App. A says "11 different LLMs") and 2 lexical metrics (EM, contains).
- **Judge output:** the single word "correct" or "incorrect".
- **Metrics:** percent agreement and Scott's π (App. F). Cohen's κ, used in an earlier version, was dropped for
  "well-documented theoretical issues" (fn. 2). App. B: κ estimates chance agreement from each rater's own
  distribution, which "adjusts for the observed average differences between raters, which is in fact part of what we
  intend to measure". The two coincide when the raters' marginals are equal.

## Evaluation
- **Sample:** 400 random questions, each answered by all 9 exam-takers (§3). App. I: 5 subsamples of 300 give a
  Scott's π standard deviation of at most 0.0126 for the LLM judges listed (GPT-4 is not in that table; EM 0.29).
- **Human ground truth:** CS graduate students. Inter-human agreement was measured on 1,200 answers (600 from
  Llama-2 7B, 600 from Llama-3.1 70B): Scott's π 96.2 ± 1.07 and 98.52% ± 0.42% agreement (§3, App. A). After that,
  each sample had one human.
- **Alignment (§4.1, Fig. 1b):** Scott's π is in the high 80s only for Llama-3 70B, Llama-3.1 70B and GPT-4 Turbo. The
  best judge is "8 points behind human judgment". Llama-3 8B has π 59 while its percent agreement is "still well above
  80%". Above π 80 the score gap is up to 5 points; judges above 90% agreement "may still differ more than 10 points".
- **Error types (Table 2, 900 annotated Llama-2 7B outputs), recall of GPT-4 / Llama-3 70B:**
  - incorrect entity: 98.3% / 96.6%
  - under-specified: 33.9% / 23.3%
  - too few entities: 80.0% / 60.0%
  - too many entities: 90.1% / 90.1%
- **Dummy answers (§5.3, Fig. 5):** some judges accept "Yes" and "Sure" as correct. They also do not always accept a
  verbatim gold answer.
- **Leniency (§5.4, App. O).** In their model, the judge is right with probability P_c and otherwise says "correct" with
  probability P+. "P+ for most models is significantly higher than 0.5". Examples: GPT-4 0.69, Llama-3 70B 0.90,
  Llama-2 70B 0.99. JudgeLM 7B (0.19) is the exception.
- **Cost:** about 200 GPU-hours, USD 125 of OpenAI credits and 75 human annotation hours (App. H).

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us the audit metrics and the bias to watch. It takes no territory.
- **Same setting as our audit.** Our scorer audit has `claude-sonnet-5.5` judge typed answers against a reference. That
  is their reference-based correct/incorrect setting, not pairwise preference.
- **One deviation.** Our judge is told the reference "may be wrong", and theirs treats the reference as correct. State
  this as a design difference.
- **Metric.** EXP-0008 and EXP-0009 report percent agreement per stratum and Cohen's κ (0.889; 0.860). **Add Scott's
  π**, computed from the row-level audit file on the same rows as κ, and cite App. B for why.
  - Caveat: the audit sample is stratified (right-scored and wrong-scored rows drawn separately). π and κ then describe
    the audit sample, not the full run.
- **Leniency.** A lenient judge produces "system right, scorer wrong" disagreements. Our registered rule already
  handles this: every disagreement is re-checked against the plant graph, and the graph decides (`review-fixes.md`
  §5.3). In EXP-0009 all 25 disagreements were judge errors, so the graph-checked verdict equals the scorer verdict.
  That makes P+ (App. O) estimable from the audit 2×2 table.
- **List answers.** Their weakest category is *under-specified* or *too few entities* answers. That matches our
  partial valve and instrument lists, so judge recall on incomplete isolation sets is the risk to report.
- It is not about the bootstrap, the decoys or the families.

## Limitations they admit (App. A)
- "Simplicity of the task": real judge use is more complex, so caution is needed beyond it.
- The human-alignment estimate initially came from one exam-taker model; it was later extended to two.
- 400 samples, limited by cost.
- Other judges were not tried.

## Reusable
Scott's π (App. F, Eq. 2 in App. B), the P_c / P+ leniency estimator (App. O), the dummy-answer probes (§5.3), and
the error-type recall table (Table 2) as a template for judge errors by answer type.
