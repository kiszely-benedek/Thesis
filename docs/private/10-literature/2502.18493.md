## Claim
Detect **and correct** errors in P&IDs automatically by matching hand-written rule graphs against the P&ID graph. Prior work only detected; this one repairs.

## Method
P&IDs → graphs via **pyDEXPI**. Nodes are equipment/instruments with type, tag and function codes; edges are piping connections with diameter, material, direction.

A **rule graph** is a pattern pair:
- **LHS (pattern)** — a connected subgraph encoding an *incorrect* local structure
- **RHS (replacement)** — the corrected structure

Matching is **subgraph isomorphism via VF2** (Cordella et al. 2004). Tractable because P&ID graphs are small — typically <1000 nodes. *(Note that assumption. It is a per-sheet assumption, and it is one of the things that breaks at plant scale.)*

Correction is a fixed-point loop: match → delete the faulty subgraph → insert the corrected one → propagate attributes → repeat until no rule matches. Idempotent, and handles cascading fixes.

**33 rules** across four categories: connection errors, instrumentation errors, valve configuration errors, labeling/tag inconsistencies. Five worked examples — missing check valve after a pump; redundant isolation valves; pressure instrument on the wrong side; crossed piping without a junction; ungrounded equipment.

## Evaluation
A case study on **one illustrative P&ID** with seeded errors. High detection precision, most corrections need no intervention, matching runs in milliseconds. No large-scale quantitative benchmark.

## Relation to this dissertation
**This is the consistency/validation baseline — do not reinvent it.** The "insufficient or incorrect information" strand of the project should extend this rather than start over.

Two openings:
1. Their rules are **intra-sheet**. Cross-sheet consistency — a dangling off-page connector, a tag referenced on sheet 12 but defined nowhere, mismatched line specs across a cut — is a rule class they cannot express. That is ours.
2. VF2 subgraph isomorphism at "<1000 nodes" does not obviously survive 38,000. Whether rule checking scales is an empirical question worth a paragraph.

## Limitations they admit
Rules are hand-engineered, so coverage equals what someone thought to write; novel error patterns are missed; attribute-level errors get less treatment than topological ones; assumes DEXPI-compliant input, so legacy raster diagrams need prior OCR/vectorization — **exactly our Phase 5 input**.

Future work they name: learning rules from data, temporal/version consistency, interactive engineer-in-the-loop correction.

## Reusable
The rule-graph formalism (LHS/RHS + VF2) transfers directly to cross-sheet rules. The four-category taxonomy. Their seeded-error methodology is a good model for generating known defects in our synthetic benchmark.
