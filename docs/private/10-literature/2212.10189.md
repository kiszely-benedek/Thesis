## Claim
The first KBQA benchmark with unanswerable questions. Three state-of-the-art models lose accuracy on them even after
adaptation, and "often detect unanswerability for wrong reasons" (Abstract).

## Method
**Definition (§2).** A question is unanswerable for KB G if it has no valid logical form (label **NK**), or if its
valid form returns an empty answer (label **NA**).

**Construction (§3).** KB elements are deleted from GrailQA's Freebase in the order types → relations → entities →
facts. The deletion is sampled by inverse popularity. A question is marked unanswerable only when all its answer paths
are broken.
- **Mentioned-entity deletion** invalidates the logical form (NK).
- **Fact deletion and other-entity deletion** leave the form valid but the answer empty (NA).
- The target is "33%" unanswerable, split "nearly equally (8.25%)" over the four categories.
- Unanswerable test questions are 47% i.i.d. and 53% zero-shot.

**Models.** RnG-KBQA, ReTraCk and TIARA, each with or without thresholds (+T). They are trained on answerable
questions only (A) or on both (A+U).

**Metrics.** Logical-form EM, plus answer F1, both regular (R) and lenient (L).

## Evaluation
- **Data (Tables 1, 2, 7, 8):** 50,507 questions.
  | Split | Answerable | NK | NA |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Train | 23,933 | 7,110 | 4,240 |
  | Dev | 3,399 | 1,064 | 595 |
  | Test | 6,808 | 2,162 | 1,196 |
  - The test set is therefore 3,358 of 10,166 unanswerable, which is 33.0%. That figure is my sum, and the Table 7
    per-category counts give the same total.
- **Gold:** produced by the deletion procedure. No human checking is reported.
- **Runs:** "our results are based on single runs" (Limitations).
- **Headline (Table 3):** the best overall EM is 72.6 (TIARA+T, A+U), "6.5 percentage points lower than the best
  answerable performance (79.2 EM for TIARA)" (§5, RQ1 C).
- **Right answer, wrong reason (Table 4, RQ2 C).** On missing facts, RnG-KBQA+T (A+U) has F1(R) 86.0 but EM 32.5. On
  other missing entities it has 87.1 / 30.8. These are "correct A = NA predictions for the wrong reason".
- **False abstention (RQ1 C).** The gains on unanswerable questions cost answerable ones, because "the models
  incorrectly predict l = NK for answerable questions".
- **Partial zero-shot is harder than full zero-shot** by 15.7 EM points for RnG-KBQA+T (RQ3 B, Table 6).

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us the decoy taxonomy and two diagnostics. It takes no territory.
- **Mapping.** `UNANSWERABLE_TAG` (a well-formed tag absent from the plant) has the signature of **mentioned-entity
  missing → NK**. Table 4 groups that with the schema-missing columns. `NO_PATH` (two real items, no flow path) has
  the signature of **valid query, empty answer → NA**.
- **Caveat on the mapping.** Their negatives come from KB *incompleteness*; completing the KB answers them. Ours are
  true negatives in a complete ground-truth graph, so they are closer to **false-premise** questions. Their
  Limitations section names false premise as out of scope. State that the analogy is structural, not semantic.
- **Right for the wrong reason.** Our `not_present` is scored without checking how it was reached. From the traces,
  report the share of correct abstentions that followed an actual tag lookup or reachability check. That is our
  analogue of their EM-vs-F1 gap.
- **False-abstention rate.** RQ1 C is the published precedent for reporting abstention on answerable questions next
  to decoy accuracy.
- **Decoy share.** Theirs is 33% by design. State ours and keep the decoys in their own column.
- It is not about the bootstrap or the judge.

## Limitations they admit
- The deletion method "limits the nature of unanswerable questions. All of these become answerable by completing the
  provided KB". False premise and irrelevant questions are not covered.
- Single runs: "generating multiple results … was not possible".
- The dataset "is one sample dataset with unanswerability".

## Reusable
The NK/NA split as a reporting axis, the "wrong reason" diagnostic (abstention correct while the trace or logical form
is wrong), and the false-abstention framing.

(2,162 of 8,970, Table 2)". The 2,162 is only the NK column. With the NA column (1,196), the test set has 3,358
unanswerable of 10,166 (33.0%), which matches the stated 33% target.
