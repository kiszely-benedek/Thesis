# Liptak IEH Process Control (1995) vs generator v2.1

Why it matters: the control schemes of generator v2 (design §7, marked [U] = "textbook practice that no source read in this project codifies") can now be grounded in a cited handbook, scheme by scheme.

## §8.23 Pump controls (pp. 1362-1386)
- Capacity control of pumps: (1) control valve on the discharge, (2) on-off switching, (3) speed, (4) stroke adjustment; which ones apply depends on pump type.
- **Reciprocating pumps**: control by stroke length, stroke speed or stroke interval; the pulsed discharge makes them **"not suited to control by throttling valves"**. Metering pumps: discharge pressure must stay above suction pressure (piping or a **back-pressure regulator** on the discharge); pulsation dampener ≥ 5 × stroke volume; calibration tank / level-calibrated drawdown. → confirms Toghraei pp. 194, 314: GEN U8 dosing pumps with a ratio FV on the discharge (no-VFD variant) is a DEVIATION; fix = stroke/speed control or recycle, plus BPR.
- **Rotary pumps** (gear, lobe, screw, vane): **a relief valve is always provided** (to pump suction or to the feed tank; rupture disc for slurries); continuous capacity control by a **pressure-controlled bypass in combination with a flow control valve**, or by speed. → GEN U4 rotary transfer FIC→FV is acceptable only together with a PCV bypass back to the tank (or VFD); relief to suction ✓.
- **Centrifugal pumps**: on-off level control with LSH/LSL (pump-down), HOA switch, running light; dual pumps with an alternator, LSHH starts both; spare started by a low-pressure switch; second pump started on pressure, stopped on flow. Throttling with a control valve on the discharge + **minimum-flow bypass with a back-pressure regulator (pressure "kickback")**; rule of thumb ~1 GPM per pump hp; if no bypass, a **low-flow switch (FSL) stops the pump**. → adds two min-flow variants (PCV kickback; FSL trip) beside RO and FIC (Toghraei pp. 176-179).
- Variable-speed pumping preferred in friction-dominated systems; payback if average load ≤ 80 %.
- Water hammer: slow-closing valves, bypass around fast-closing check valves, surge tanks/accumulators.

## §8.9 Compressor controls (pp. 1123-1150)
- Capacity control by type (Table 8.9a): **rotary** (lobe, screw, vane, liquid ring) — speed, suction/bypass/vent throttling, bypass back to suction on pressure control; liquid-ring vacuum service by bleeding gas into suction on pressure control. **Reciprocating** — on-off by pressure switches with a receiver; **step unloading** (3-step suction-valve unloaders, 5-step with clearance pocket) driven by a pressure controller/switches via a solenoid; a **discharge-to-suction bypass throttling valve** when exact pressure is needed. **Centrifugal/axial** — discharge throttling (least efficient), suction throttling, inlet guide vanes, speed (most efficient).
- **Anti-surge** is described only for dynamic (centrifugal/axial) machines: flow sensor on the **suction** (or corrected discharge Δp), ΔP transmitter across the machine, ratio/bias relay generating the setpoint, FIC driving a **fail-open globe surge (recycle) valve**, fast actuator (booster, positioner, solenoids), **redundant parallel surge valves recommended**, backup interlocks FSL / FSLL that open the valve fully, overrides through a low selector (high discharge pressure PIC, user-shutdown mass balance), PIC load loop interacting with the surge loop.
  → GEN U2 draws the anti-surge FT→FIC→FV recycle for every compressor class → for Reciprocating/Rotary classes replace by the type-specific capacity control (unloaders on a PC / pressure-controlled bypass); centrifugal keeps anti-surge (FO surge valve).
- Series compressors on one shaft: one anti-surge system; separate shafts: separate systems; parallel compressors: **each its own anti-surge**, **check valves prevent backflow into idle units**, load sharing by FFIC.
- A **check valve in the discharge line as close to the compressor as possible**; recycle valve fast-opening, slow-closing. → GEN ✓ (check), FO attribute missing.
- Lube-oil system: dual pumps, low level / low pressure / high temperature alarms, head tank (usually a separate P&ID); dry gas seals with inert gas. Optimisation: valve-position control of header pressure, low-suction-pressure and motor-load (kW) overrides on the speed controller. → GEN: IT→II on compressor exists, override not drawn.

## §8.20 Heat exchanger, condenser and evaporator controls (pp. 1305-1320)
- Liquid-liquid: TIC on the process outlet throttles the **utility/coolant** valve; inlet location usually preferred; positioner and **equal-percentage trim** recommended (process gain varies with load; tune at minimum load). **Bypass control** for speed: a three-way valve (diverting upstream preferred, mixing downstream) or **two two-way valves with opposite failure positions**; a **manual balancing valve in the bypass**; three-way valves not above 260 °C or 167 °C differential. Throttling the coolant on its own outlet temperature to save water gives a limit cycle → avoid. → GEN U5 bypass TV on the last exchanger, steam TV on heaters → CONSISTENT; the balancing valve, opposite-fail pair and FO/FC attributes are missing.
- Steam heaters: TIC → steam inlet valve + steam trap (rangeability problem → large and small valve in parallel); control valve in the condensate line gives asymmetric, poor control; better: **condensate pot with level control** (setpoint floated by a VPC), pumping/lifting trap, or **bypass on the process side** with steam held at constant condensing pressure; **cascade TIC → steam pressure/flow controller** (pressure regulator as a fast slave). → GEN 6.14 (TV + SteamTrap) is the simplest variant; condensate-pot variant optional.
- Parallel heaters near boiling need **flow distribution control**.
- Condensers: throttle cooling water (residence time ≤ 1 min, velocity > 1.35 m/s, recirculating pump), or vary wetted surface by **partial flooding** (preferred; load seen as condensate level); air coolers by **louvers / blade pitch / fan speed**; refrigerated condenser: LIC on refrigerant level sets area, PIC on refrigerant vapour sets temperature; inerts purge; vacuum by steam ejector with PIC on motive steam and **bleed-air/gas PCV** before the ejector. → GEN: column pressure via drum vapour PV only (one variant); air-cooler fan/louver control missing.
- Reboilers: one degree of freedom → one controller on the heat input (steam flow or BTU control); parallel reboilers need load balancing.
- **Fired reboiler**: TRC on reboiler return throttles the fuel TV (FC); TAH; draft gauge PI with manual stack damper; stack and firebox TI; **tower-bottom LSL and fuel-gas PSL trip a manual-reset solenoid that vents the fuel TV** (the trip acts on the control valve, no separate XV).
- **Crude heater**: feed FRC; **pass flows split by remote-manual HV per pass with FI per pass** and TI per pass; outlet TRC on fuel; burner-header PIC; fuel-oil atomising steam control; excess-O2 / draft control with manual stack damper.

## §8.19 Furnace and reformer controls (keyword reading)
- Process heater: outlet TRC → fuel gas TV (FC, single-seated for tight shutoff); hazards = loss of process flow and flame failure; **FSL on process flow, BS (flame) and PSL (fuel) trip a manual-reset solenoid FY venting the fuel TV**; draft gauge and stack damper set by the operator. Mixed fuel (gas preferred, oil trim) with PSL cutting gas; excess-O2 analyser sets draft; low-O2 and high-draft limits.
- → GEN U3 (TIC → fuel TV, PIC, BE→BSL→separate XV) is consistent in kind; Liptak's form (solenoid venting the control valve) and Toghraei's form (separate, often double SDVs, pp. 213-215) are **alternatives** → sample. Missing in GEN: FSL on process (pass) flow, per-pass FI/HV, O2 analyser.

## §8.12 Distillation basic controls (partial)
- Degrees of freedom; material-balance vs energy-balance control; DV, LV configurations.
- **Pairing rules** (table "Dynamic response and sensitivity limitations on pairing"): e.g. accumulator level not by distillate flow when the reflux ratio L/D is low (use reflux), not by reflux when L/D is high (use distillate); bottoms level not by reboiler heat when the column is wide; bottoms level not by bottoms flow when V/B is large; bottoms composition not by reflux in tall columns; both compositions not by material-balance manipulations.
- **Tray temperature** infers composition: lower-section temperature **cascaded to the reboiler steam flow controller**; upper-section temperature cascaded to reflux; the pressure control point near the temperature control point. Analysers cascaded to temperature, with dead-time compensation, low-select constraints.
- → GEN U1 (drum LIC → distillate, reflux FIC, bottoms LIC → bottoms, tray TIC → reboiler steam, column PIC on drum vapour) = the standard **LV configuration** → CONSISTENT and now sourced; the alternative pairing (accumulator level → reflux for low-reflux columns, DV configuration) and the TIC→FIC cascade are candidates for sampled variants.

## §8.24 Reactors (keyword reading)
- Exothermic reactors: **cascade reactor TIC → jacket (outlet) TIC** with a **jacket recirculation pump** (constant jacket dead time, high heat-transfer coefficient); equal-percentage coolant valve; split-range heating/cooling with fail positions (heating closes, coolant opens on failure); runaway protection. → GEN U3 is a tubular/fixed-bed style reactor with inlet TIC via feed/effluent bypass → consistent for that type; a stirred jacketed reactor pattern (U8 agitated vessel) lacks jacket temperature control.

## §8.4 Blending (keyword reading)
- In-line blending: each component **ratioed to the total blend flow** (or to a wild stream), ratio relays, preset **totalizer with automatic shutdown** for batching, analyser trim of the ratio. → GEN U8 FFIC + AT→AIC → CONSISTENT; totaliser-batch variant optional.

## What this adds to v2.2
1. Sources for the [U] control schemes of design §7: U1 (LV configuration, temperature→steam cascade), U2 (anti-surge for dynamic machines only; unloaders / bypass for PD), U3 (fuel TIC, trips, pass flow), U5 (bypass control, balancing valve), U8 (ratio to total/wild flow, analyser trim), pumps (four capacity-control methods by pump type).
2. Rule corrections: no throttling FV on reciprocating-pump discharge (also Toghraei); rotary-pump FV only with a PCV bypass; anti-surge only on Centrifugal/Axial compressors.
3. Sampled alternatives: min-flow (RO / FIC / PCV kickback / FSL trip); trip form (solenoid on the control valve vs separate SDV); column level pairing (LV vs reflux-on-level); steam heater (inlet TV + trap vs condensate pot LIC); exchanger bypass (3-way vs two 2-way with opposite fail).
4. Valve failure positions (FO surge valves, FC fuel valves, opposite-fail bypass pairs) — same attribute gap as Toghraei and Liptak Vol. 1.
