## Claim
"KBQA models should have three levels of built-in generalization": i.i.d., compositional and zero-shot (Abstract,
§2.2). The paper releases GrailQA (64,331 questions) and a BERT-based ranking model.

## Method
**Levels (§2.2).**
- **I.i.d.:** every schema item has been seen, and "the test questions follow the training distribution".
- **Compositional:** every schema item has been seen, but "the specific logical form" has not.
- **Zero-shot:** at least one unseen schema item.
- **Orthogonal axis:** "language variation, i.e., different paraphrases corresponding to the same logical form".

**Data pipeline (§3.1).**
1. Canonical logical forms are generated from Freebase, with up to 4 relations and an optional count, superlative or
   comparative function.
2. A graduate student validates each form: "could we reasonably expect a real human user to ask this question?" The
   "overall approval rate at this step is 86.5%".
3. A graduate student writes the canonical question, and another student cross-validates it.
4. AMT paraphrases follow, with 5 valid paraphrases per question. Cross-validation discards a paraphrase whose fidelity
   approval is below 75% or fluency approval below 60%; "17.4% of paraphrases are discarded".
5. Controlled sampling and entity re-grounding produce the final questions.

## Evaluation
- **Data (§3.2, Table 1):** 4,969 canonical logical forms, 29,457 paraphrases, 64,331 questions; 86 domains, 1,534
  classes, 3,720 relations, 32,585 entities. Contributors: 11 graduate students and 6,685 crowd workers.
- **Relations per question:** 1 = 44,340, 2 = 16,610, 3 = 3,254, 4 = 127 (§3.2).
- **Gold:** executable logical forms.
- **Quality check (§3.2):** 100 sampled canonical logical forms. "3 of the 100 canonical questions" and "12 of the 576
  paraphrases" mismatched, i.e. "3% error rate of canonical question annotation, 2.1% error rate of paraphrasing, and
  5.6% error rate overall". How 5.6% is combined is not stated.
- **Splits (§5.1):** 44,337 / 6,763 / 13,231. Dev and test are 50% zero-shot, 25% compositional and 25% i.i.d.
- **Results (Table 3, Ranking model, EM / F1):** overall 50.6 / 58.0; i.i.d. 59.9 / 67.0; compositional 45.5 / 53.9;
  zero-shot 48.6 / 55.7. Runs per model are not stated.
- **§5.3.2:** "The performance of all models degrades rapidly as questions become more complex" (Fig. 4, by number of
  relations; values only in the figure).

**Claimed vs demonstrated.** The three-level split is demonstrated: the level gaps appear in Table 3. Naturalness
rests on one graduate-student filter and a 100-item audit.

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us vocabulary and a precedent, and takes no territory (no P&IDs, no corpus scale).
- **Held-out families.** Our sealed held-out families recombine seen relations (flow, signal, control, unit). That is
  the **compositional** level, not zero-shot. Our held-out wordings test the orthogonal **language-variation** axis.
  Claim exactly that, and no more.
- **Question families and generator gold.** It is the KGQA precedent for gold computed by executing a program. Our
  reference functions over the generator's `networkx` graph are the same idea without the human steps. We have no
  naturalness filter and no paraphrase stage. Name both as differences.
- **Strata.** Their 1–4-relation distribution (44,340 → 127) has the same long-tail shape as our high-`k` bins. It
  supports stratified (round-robin) sampling instead of natural frequencies.
- **Human spot check.** The §3.2 audit (100 items, per-stage error rates) is the template for reporting ours.
  EXP-0009's 48-question gold spot check with 0 defects should be reported in the same form: sample size, rule,
  checker, and the error rate with its interval.
- It is not about the bootstrap, the decoys or the LLM judge.

## Limitations they admit (§5.4, §7)
- "more sophisticated, context-sensitive entity linkers … are needed".
- "More intelligently guided search is a promising future direction".
- On BERT, "there is still a lack of deeper understanding on why that is the case".

## Reusable
The three level definitions (§2.2), the naturalness criterion (§3.1), and the quality-audit reporting form (§3.2).
