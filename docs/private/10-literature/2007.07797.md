## Claim
For nested data (subjects → neurons), the hierarchical bootstrap keeps the Type-I error at its nominal level and keeps
more power than summarising per subject. Traditional tests that treat all points as independent "can result in a false
positive rate of over 45%" (Abstract).

## Method
- **Procedure (§2.1, Fig. 1d):** "resampling with replacement first at the level of subjects followed by neurons
  within a subject". The statistic is the mean across all resampled neurons. N_bootstrap = 10⁴.
- **Testing (§2.2):** p_boot is the share of bootstrap means on one side of the null.
- **Rule for designing the resampling (§2.3):** "come as close as possible to replicating what one might expect if
  they had physically repeated that experiment".
- **Design effect (§2.5):** DEFF = 1 + ρ(n_j − 1), where ρ is the intra-cluster correlation (ICC).
- **Comparators:** "Traditional" (all points independent), "Summarized" (one mean per subject), and a linear mixed
  model (LMM).

## Evaluation
Simulations of Poisson firing at 5 Hz, plus two re-analyses of real data (songbird pitch, fly optogenetics). Each
simulation condition was repeated 1,000 or 10,000 times.
- **Hierarchical data (§3.1.1, Fig. 2).** 1,000 subjects × 100 neurons, with a 3 Hz per-subject offset.
  - The Traditional false-positive rate rises from about 46% to almost 96% as neurons per subject grow.
  - Bootstrap, Summarized and LMM are "not significantly different from 0.05".
- **Independent data (§3.1.2, Fig. 3).** The bootstrap is conservative here.
  - False-positive rate: 0.66 ± 0.08% for the bootstrap, against 4.80 ± 0.21% (Traditional), 4.99 ± 0.22%
    (Summarized) and 4.80 ± 0.21% (LMM).
  - SEM: 1.407 ± 0.035 × 10⁻² against about 1.00 × 10⁻², i.e. "roughly 1.4 times larger".
- **Few subjects (§3.1.3, Fig. 4b; N = 1–16 subjects per group).** The bootstrap is anti-conservative here.
  - At N = 1 its false-positive rate equals the Traditional one, because "the bootstrap assumes that neural level data
    is the true distribution".
  - The rate "tends towards a 5% error rate with increasing number of subjects".
  - The LMM at N = 1 gives 16 ± 1%, "well below that of the bootstrap".
  - The values at N = 5 appear **only in the figure**, and I could not read them from the text.
- **Warning (§3.1.3):** the bootstrap "can weight trials within levels more heavily than one would expect if the
  number of samples in the upper levels is very low … one must therefore be mindful".

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us our exact scheme and its two failure modes. It takes no territory.
- **Mapping.** Plant = subject; question = neuron; arm or scale = experimental condition. Our two-stage cluster
  bootstrap (plants with replacement, then questions within each drawn plant) is Fig. 1d.
- **Statistic.** Theirs is a pooled mean over all neurons. Ours is an equal-weight mean over plants. With equal `n` per
  plant (235 in scope R) the two coincide, and with unequal `n` they differ. State which one we use.
- **Two opposite biases at G = 5.**
  - **Conservative**, about 1.4× wider, when the plant effect is near zero (§3.1.2). EXP-0007's plant SD of about
    0.010, "below the question noise" (`review-fixes.md` §5.1), is close to this case.
  - **Anti-conservative** with very few top-level units (§3.1.3, Fig. 4b), because within-plant questions get too much
    weight.
  - The paper does not tell us which bias dominates at N = 5. The free coverage simulation recommended in
    `question-set-methodology-grounding.md` §5 is the way to settle it.
- **§2.3's rule** justifies the two stages: a repeat of our experiment draws new plant seeds *and* new questions.
- **DEFF** shows why 235 questions per plant inflate variance even at a small ICC. Compute ρ from the saved
  per-question results rather than assume it.
- It is not about the decoys, the gold or the judge.

## Limitations they admit
"if the data collected do not accurately represent the dynamics of the underlying distribution, the bootstrap cannot
provide accurate estimates" (§3.1.3). They also say the resampling "may and should change based on the hypothesis
being tested" (§2.3).
