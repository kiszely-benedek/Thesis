## Claim
A chatbot over DEXPI P&IDs. Condensing the knowledge graph before giving it to the LLM improves answers and cuts cost.

## Method
DEXPI XML → **pyDEXPI** → NetworkX → **Neo4j LPG**. Node labels come from pyDEXPI class names; properties hold specifications. Two relationship families: **domain** (`has_*`, `is_located_in`) and **lexical** (`send_to`, `control`, `send_signal_to`, `measured_by`, `is_logical_end_of`).

Condensation in three steps — prune domain/connection nodes, condense low-information nodes, remove non-process properties:

> **212 nodes / 405 relationships → 53 nodes / 57 relationships. ~67,000 → ~9,000 tokens (86% reduction).**

The condensed graph is exported to GraphML and pasted whole into the system prompt (LangChain, with chat history). **No retrieval at all.**

## Evaluation
**One flowsheet** (one tank, two pumps, two heat exchangers). **Three questions**: process tracing, "list all valves", safety recommendations. Models: Claude Sonnet 3.5, GPT-4o, Claude Haiku 3.

Condensation improved process tracing by ~20%. Both graph versions retrieved all 11 valves. Sonnet 3.5 gave the most flowsheet-specific safety recommendations.

## Relation to this dissertation
The v1 of ChatP&ID — same group, same stack, superseded by 2603.22528. Read it for the **condensation algorithm**, which is described more plainly here than in the newer paper, and cite it as the origin of the abstraction-level idea.

Its whole design is prompt-stuffing, which makes it the clearest illustration of the assumption this dissertation breaks: it only works because the graph is tiny.

## Limitations they admit
Hallucinations against a domain with "low tolerance for mistakes"; smaller LLMs cannot read the graph format; a single flowsheet evaluated; no comprehensive metrics. They name "AI-assisted HAZOP" as the goal.

## Reusable
The condensation steps; the domain/lexical relationship split — a clean schema distinction worth keeping in our own graph model.
