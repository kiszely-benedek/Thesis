ACL 2018 short paper. arXiv v1, 11 Jun 2018. Fn. 1 says the ACL version called the dataset "SQUADRUN". Full PDF read
2026-11-28, §1–§6 and App. A.

## Claim
SQuAD 2.0 adds 53,775 crowd-written unanswerable questions that "look similar to answerable ones". The best model
reaches only 66.3 F1, against a human 89.5 (§1).

## Method
**Two desiderata (§2).**
- **Relevance:** otherwise "simple heuristics (e.g., based on word overlap)" separate the negatives.
- **Existence of plausible answers:** some span of the asked type exists. Otherwise "type-matching heuristics" separate
  them.

**Collection (§4.1).** Daemo crowdworkers wrote up to five unanswerable questions per paragraph. They were paid
$10.50/h and spent 7 min per paragraph.

**Scoring (fn. 3).** "For negative examples, abstaining receives a score of 1, and any other response gets 0". Models
abstain above a no-answer probability threshold that is tuned on dev (§5.1).

## Evaluation
- **Data (Table 2):** train 130,319 (43,498 negatives); dev 11,873 (5,945); test 8,862 (4,332). Dev and test are
  roughly 1:1, train roughly 2:1 answerable to unanswerable.
- **Human accuracy (§4.2):** crowdworkers answered all dev and test questions, 4.8 answers per question on average,
  majority vote.
- **Gold audit (§4.3):** 100 random dev negatives were inspected, and "93% of the sampled negative examples are indeed
  unanswerable". Types (Table 1): negation 9%, antonym 20%, entity swap 21%, mutual exclusion 15%, impossible
  condition 4%, other neutral 24%, answerable (noise) 7%.
- **Main results (Table 3, test EM / F1):** BNA 59.2 / 62.1, DocQA 59.3 / 62.3, DocQA+ELMo 63.4 / 66.3, human
  86.9 / 89.5. The "baseline that always abstains gets 48.9 test F1" (§5.2).
- **Automatic negatives (§5.3, Table 4):** the best F1 is 83.0 with TF-IDF negatives and 89.6 with rule-based ones,
  against 67.6 on SQuAD 2.0 dev. That is "15.4 F1 points lower", "suggesting that automatically generated negative
  examples are much easier".
- **Distractors (§5.4, Table 5):** "roughly half of all wrong answers on unanswerable questions exactly matched the
  plausible answers", for systems and humans alike.
- **Runs:** one per model, not stated otherwise.

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us the scoring rule and the counter-evidence for our decoys. It takes no territory.
- **Scoring.** Our `not_present` rule for `UNANSWERABLE_TAG` and `NO_PATH` is fn. 3. Add the **always-abstain
  baseline** per column (their 48.9). It shows what a system earns by refusing everything, and it pairs with our
  false-abstention rate.
- **Our decoys are automatic, i.e. rule-based, negatives.** `UNANSWERABLE_TAG` is an entity swap built with the
  generator's tag grammar. It meets **relevance** (plant grammar, real unit numbers). It meets **plausible answer**
  only weakly, since items of the asked class exist. One tag lookup still rejects it. §5.3's 15.4-point gap is the
  published reason to call these decoys easy. `NO_PATH` is closer to "impossible condition" and needs an exhaustive
  reachability check.
- **Gold.** Generator gold makes the 93% audit cheaper for us. "No path" can be proved on the ground-truth graph for
  every decoy, not for a sample of 100. Report it as a full check.
- **Distractor analysis (§5.4).** Report what a system answered when it failed a `NO_PATH` decoy, for example a route
  through a signal line or into another unit.
- It is not about the bootstrap or the judge.

## Limitations they admit
No limitations section. §3.1 describes earlier datasets: distant-supervision negatives where "65% … do not have a
plausible answer", and NewsQA where only 9.5% of questions are unanswerable.

## Reusable
The two desiderata (§2), the abstain-scores-1 rule (fn. 3), the always-abstain baseline (§5.2), the negative-type
taxonomy (Table 1), and the 100-item negative audit (§4.3).
