## Claim
CLEVR is a synthetic visual-QA dataset with "minimal biases and … detailed annotations describing the kind of reasoning
each question requires". The authors use it to diagnose model failures (Abstract).

## Method
**Question family (§3).** A family "contains a template for constructing functional programs and several text
templates providing multiple ways of expressing these programs in natural language".
- There are "90 question families, each with a single program template and an average of four text templates".
- The text templates were "manually writing one or two templates per family and then crowdsourcing question
  rewrites".

**Generation (§3).**
- Choose a family, bind its parameters, "execute the resulting program on the image's scene graph to find the answer",
  and render a text template.
- A depth-first search with scene-based pruning avoids "ill-posed" and "degenerate" questions.
- "rejection sampling to produce an approximately uniform answer distribution for each question family".

## Evaluation
- **Data (Fig. 3):** 100,000 images, 999,968 questions, 853,554 unique. Train / val / test: 70,000 / 15,000 / 15,000
  images; 699,989 / 149,991 / 149,988 questions.
- **Gold:** program execution on the ground-truth scene graph.
- **Human baseline (§4.1):** 5,500 test questions on Mechanical Turk, majority of three workers.
- **Protocol (§4.1):** "All experiments were designed on the validation set; after finalizing the design we ran each
  model once on the test set."
- **Slicing by program:** results are reported by question type, relation type, chain vs tree topology, and size.
  - Example: CNN+LSTM+SA scores 93% on spatial vs 78% on same-attribute query questions, and 92% on chain vs 74% on
    tree questions (§4.3–§4.4).
- **Effective size (§4.5, Fig. 7).** Accuracy "appears unrelated to question size" (the number of functions). The
  *effective* question is "the smallest program that … gives the same answer", and "the error rate of all models
  increases with effective question size".
- **Shortcut detection (§4.6).** Re-executing the program with modified semantics (absolute image halves) finds the
  questions answerable by a shortcut. They are then excluded.
- **Confound control (fn. 2, fn. 4).** Same-attribute questions are excluded from the size plot because of "unwanted
  correlations between size and difficulty". The spatial plot is restricted to chain questions "to avoid unwanted
  correlations between question topology and number of relationships".

## Relation to this dissertation
It hands us the vocabulary and three checks. It takes no territory.
- **Question families.** Define ours as CLEVR does: a reference program over the generator's ground-truth graph plus a
  wording. One difference to state: CLEVR averages four text templates per family, while ours have one dev wording,
  plus one sealed held-out wording for ten families.
- **Generator gold.** "Execute the program on the ground-truth structure" is CLEVR's method, and it is ours.
- **Bias control.** Rejection sampling for uniform answers is the general form of our yes/no alternation in
  `CONNECTED` and `SAME_UNIT`. Our per-anchor cap, untagged refusal and availability gate play the role of the
  ill-posed and degenerate filter.
- **Effective `k`.** Our `k` counts sheet-boundary crossings in the reference evidence. That is a *nominal* size. A
  CLEVR-style effective version is the fewest crossings an answer actually needs, or whether a shortcut answers it
  without crossing (the `LOOP_ACTUATED_VALVE` loop-tag case, ADR-0044 U2). That is the §4.5–§4.6 check, run by
  re-executing the reference functions with modified semantics.
- **The family–`k` confound.** fn. 4 is the published precedent: report `k` curves within one family or one path type,
  not pooled.
- **Protocol.** Design on validation, run once on test. That supports our seal-and-freeze, and it is equally
  one-run-per-question.
- It is not about the bootstrap, the decoys or the judge.

## Limitations they admit
"accuracy on CLEVR is not an end goal in itself: a hand-crafted system with explicit knowledge of the CLEVR universe
might work well, but will not generalize to real-world settings" (§1). This is the synthetic-plant threat for us: a
Cypher arm that knows the generator's schema is that hand-crafted system.

## Reusable
The family definition (§3), rejection sampling (§3), the effective-size and shortcut re-execution analyses (§4.5–§4.6),
and the confound-avoiding restrictions (fn. 2, fn. 4).
